# Podcast Analysis: "5 Tricks To DOMINATE Fantasy Baseball Auction Drafts"
## RotoWire Podcast — James Anderson & Marc Winokur (2026 Pre-Season)

---

## 1. The 5 Core Tricks / Strategies

### Trick 1: Spread the Wealth (Balanced Build) Over Stars & Scrubs
- The data is clear: of 13 NFBC Auction Championship winners in 2025, **none had Ohtani** and only **2 teams had even one player over $40**. Very few players over $30 across all 13 winners.
- Stars & scrubs is dying because the $1 player pool has degraded. Dollar guys are now truly scrubs: platoon bats, injured players, closer specs, prospects. You can no longer fill your roster back-end with $1 everyday players.
- Marc recommends a balanced/spread-the-wealth approach but with **differentiation** — go slightly heavier on pitching than the field to stand out.

### Trick 2: Nail the $2-$5 Buys
- Mike Maker (Marc's partner, former auction champion) says the **most important thing** is nailing the $2-$4 buys in the auction.
- These mid-to-low dollar players are where you build roster depth and find the sleepers that separate winners from losers.
- Don't pass up a good price on a player in your "green zone" early just because you're waiting for a bigger target — you may get priced out of both.

### Trick 3: Be Willing to Overpay Slightly on Foundation Players
- Marc has evolved: he's now willing to go **$5-$7 over his valuation** on big players he believes in, knowing he can make up that overpay later in the auction.
- He wants to come out of every auction with at least one first-round caliber player, overpaying the least amount possible.
- Get your big buy(s) in early to settle your build, then work the margins.

### Trick 4: Use Nomination Strategy as an Information & Price-Manipulation Tool
- Early noms (first 10): Throw out your "Plan A hinge player" to see if you'll get them. Also throw out closers/catchers for a "heat check" on the room's pricing tendencies.
- If you land a top-3 SP, immediately nominate the other top SPs to force opponents to pay full freight.
- Middle of auction: If you have purchasing power advantage (top 3 in remaining money), go "bang bang bang" nominating your targets. If you spent up early and are low on funds, nominate players you can't afford to drain others' budgets.
- Endgame: Nominate players you want. Study other teams' max bids and roster needs — if all $2+ max-bid owners already have a position filled, you can sneak your guy through at $1.

### Trick 5: Have Multiple Plans (A/B/C) and Be Agile
- Have a plan, a backup, a backup to the backup, and a backup to that.
- Make your target group as large as you can stomach.
- Don't get stubborn or locked into specific players. Be patient, nimble, and willing to pivot.
- Know how you'll handle saves before the auction starts — it's one of the trickiest allocation decisions.
- If you're strong at FAAB/waivers in certain areas, you can leave those areas lighter in the draft and vice versa.

## 3. Position-by-Position Strategy

### Catcher
- **Spend up.** Winning teams spent $22 on average, $7 more than the field.
- This year, two top-10 catchers costs $25-$30.
- Ben Rice is the hype player ($22-$27 range, went ahead of Cal Raleigh in some drafts). Marc considers him overpriced — paying more than William Contreras for an unproven catcher is risky.
- Strategy: Get one surefire top-10 catcher, pair with a $3 backup like Ryan Jeffers.

### First Base
- Good values throughout the market. Marc got Rafael Devers at $17 (went $19 in other league).
- Devers is overdrafted in snake but underpriced in auctions. Solid floor player — had a strong 2025 that flew under the radar.
- First base has enough depth that you don't need to overpay.

### Second Base
- "Not a sexy position" but Marc likes it better than 3B because of more viable options.
- Only 3-4 truly safe second basemen (Ketel Marte tier). After that, take your pick from a cluster.
- Marc paid $22 for Ketel Marte (elite) on one team, $8 for Luis Garcia (fallback) on the other.
- Lesson: Don't pass up an early good price at 2B waiting for a bigger target. Marc missed Polanco ($8), Semien ($8), and others by waiting.
- Several single-digit guys are solid: speed contributors and 4-category guys available.

### Third Base
- **Spend up.** Marc is intentionally paying $30+ here because he's "really uncomfortable" after the top tier.
- Jose Ramirez ($39): Elite 5-category contributor.
- Junior Caminero ($30): 4-category contributor.
- Both were a few dollars under AAV despite being over Marc's valuation — he was happy to overpay at a thin position.
- The drop-off after the elite tier (JRAM, Caminero, Machado, Maikel Garcia) is steep.

### Shortstop
- Lots of really good top shortstops, so Marc doesn't mind spending down.
- Willy Adames at $12 on both teams — Marc calls him "criminally underpriced."
- Key metric: Adames projects for 200+ "total" (R+RBI+SB+HR combined), and finding a 200-total guy for $12 is rare.
- Adames: 30 HR power in SF + 33 SB over past two years. The average was a problem but he settled in.

### Outfield
- Winning teams averaged $61.50 on outfield.
- Marc targets $50-$60. Last year $50 worked because of underpriced OF options; this year it's harder.
- Marc prefers NOT to spend $100 on outfield like some owners. Differentiate by spending less here.
- Plan for at least one $2-$3 OF (Jung Hoo Lee type — everyday player hitting high in lineup).
- George Springer is a recurring buy for Marc — solid veteran value.
- $1 outfielders are now strictly platoon guys or unproven prospects.

### Utility
- Use for positional flexibility and bargain hunting.

## 5. Pitching Strategy

### SP Approach
- **If you buy a top-3 SP ($40):** Very hard to also get two closers. Plan on one elite reliever + innings eaters and upside arms.
- **Spread-the-wealth SP approach:** Target Logan Webb-tier SP1 ($20), then stack high-upside arms (Burns, Woodruff, Sheehan) with one innings anchor.
- Key: Skubal is a massive safety blanket — his projected stats alone cover up ratio issues from the rest of your staff.

### Closer Strategy
- **Two closers for $25 is Marc's ideal** but increasingly hard to achieve.
- Closer inflation is real: people spending $30-40 on two closers.
- In FAB/waiver leagues: One closer in auction + find the second via FAAB is viable (8 SP + 1 RP build).
- In Draft Champions (no FAB): Lock down two closers in the auction — you won't find them in reserve rounds.
- Kade Smith at $24 is overpay; Marc would pay $21.
- Target: Clase ($15), Santana ($11) range for a two-closer build.

## 6. Nomination Strategy (Detailed)

### Early Auction (First ~10 Nominations)
1. **Throw out your "Plan A hinge player"** — the player whose acquisition determines Plan A vs Plan B. Getting price discovery here informs the entire auction.
2. **Heat check closers and catchers** — nominate a Closer 4-7 or Catcher 4 to gauge the room's spending tendencies at inflated positions.
3. **If you land a top-3 SP, immediately nominate the remaining top SPs** to ensure opponents pay full price ($40+). Don't let them sit and go for $38.
4. **Same logic for elite bats** — if you get a big bat and there are others in that tier, nominate them to make opponents pay up while the tier dries up.

### Mid-Auction
5. **If you have purchasing power (top 3 in remaining $):** Go "bang bang bang" — rapid-fire nominate your targets while you have leverage. Typically after the first break.
6. **If you spent up early (bottom 3-5 in remaining $):** Nominate players you can't afford to drain others' budgets and let the money equalize back toward you.

### Endgame
7. **Dollar days — mostly nominate players you want** since if you nom at $1 and no one bids, they're yours.
8. **Study max bids and roster needs of opponents.** If all $2+ max-bid owners already have a position filled, you can safely nominate your guy at $1.
9. **Throw out $3-$5 AAV guys you DON'T want** to drain remaining dollars from opponents before you nominate your actual targets.
10. **Don't be afraid to nominate at $2-$3** instead of $1 if the math shows you need to secure a specific player.

## 8. Balanced Build vs. Stars & Scrubs

### Marc's Clear Recommendation: Balanced / Spread the Wealth

**Why balanced wins:**
- 13/13 NFBC auction championship winners in 2025 used a balanced approach
- $1 player pool has degraded — true scrubs are platoon bats, injured players, closer specs
- Auction players have gotten much better at money management; fewer bargains fall through
- More people throughout the auction maintain purchasing power, making it harder to swoop in on deals

**Why stars & scrubs struggles now:**
- Stars & scrubs players sometimes wait too long to come back in and leave money on the table
- The "scrubs" you fill with are now genuinely bad players, not hidden sleepers
- Prospects that used to be $1 reserve-round grabs now cost real auction dollars
- Requires a very deep, reliable player list to fill the back end — most people don't have this

**Marc's twist on balanced:**
- Differentiate within the balanced framework by going slightly heavier on pitching ($100-105 vs. field at $95)
- Willing to overpay $5-7 on 1-2 foundational players he strongly believes in
- Targets 1 first-round caliber player minimum, then fills with value
- Keeps outfield spend below the field ($50-60 vs. $61.50 average) to reallocate those dollars

## 9. Adaptation Notes for 12-Team H2H Points (Fantrax)

The podcast is primarily about NFBC Roto/5x5 auctions, but the core principles translate to H2H Points with these adjustments:

- **Pitching is even MORE valuable in H2H Points** — aces win you weeks outright. The $100-105 pitching allocation may need to go even higher.
- **Saves at +5 points are massive** — closer strategy is critical. Two reliable closers at $25-30 total could be week-winners.
- **Strikeouts at +1.5 for pitchers** reward high-K arms (Skubal, Skenes, Crochet, Burns).
- **Wins at +4** reward pitchers on good teams with run support.
- **Stolen bases at +1.5** still valuable but less so than in roto where they're a whole category.
- **The -0.35 SO penalty for hitters** means contact hitters and low-K bats have more value than in standard roto.
- **Quality starts at +1.5** further reward workhouse starters — innings eaters like Webb, Nola, Flaherty gain value.
- **IP at +2.25** makes innings volume extremely important — another boost for 200-IP arms.
